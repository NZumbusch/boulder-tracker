Boulder Tracker

Plan, log and understand your climbing training.

Boulder Tracker is a training companion for boulderers and climbers, made by Nathan Zumbusch. It runs on Android and in the browser, works offline, and keeps your data on your device – there is no account and no server.

PlanTraining blocks and phases, weekly sessions from templates, goals and trips.
Live sessionsGuided sessions with an interval and hangboard timer that keeps beeping in the background.
ReadinessSleep, HRV and resting heart rate with fatigue and load, as one daily score.
AnalyticsLoad and ACWR, monotony and strain, finger load, recovery trends, benchmarks and sends.
Your dataStored on your device; optional sync through a private folder in your own Google Drive.
Android extrasHome-screen widgets, launcher shortcuts, notifications and backups.

Install

Android app (adds Google Drive sync, widgets, reminders and timer sound with the screen off). It is not in the Play Store; you download it from here.

  1. Tap Download the APK and open the file when it finishes (browser download bar or your Files app).
  2. If Android asks, allow your browser to install unknown apps, then go back and tap Install.
  3. Play Protect may say it hasn't seen this app before, because it isn't from the Play Store. Tap More details → Install anyway.
  4. Updates arrive inside the app (Settings → About → App updates), each checked against its checksum first.

Want proof it's the code on GitHub? Check the build yourself.

iPhone / iPad: open this page in Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen. In Chrome or Firefox on iOS, use the browser's menu or Share sheet for the same entry. (Links opened inside Reddit or other apps can't install; open them in Safari first.) iOS has no sync, reminders or widgets.

Any browser: just open the web app; it also works offline once loaded.

Is this APK safe? Check it yourself

The app is open source: github.com/NZumbusch/boulder-tracker. The APK is built by GitHub's servers from that code on every release, not on a personal computer, and each build records which commit it came from. You don't have to take my word for it:

On a computer, download the APK and compare:

sha256sum boulder-tracker.apk
apksigner verify --print-certs boulder-tracker.apk
gh attestation verify boulder-tracker.apk --repo NZumbusch/boulder-tracker
  • sha256sum must print the SHA-256 above. The app does the same check on every update before it lets Android install anything.
  • gh attestation verify asks GitHub to confirm this exact file was produced by the build workflow in that repository, from the commit shown. Builds made before attestations were added won't have one; the next promoted build will.
  • apksigner shows who signed it. The certificate is the developer's own key (Android labels such keys "Android Debug"), not a Play Store key; its fingerprint above never changes between versions, and Android refuses an update signed with a different one.

The build recipe is the public file .github/workflows/deploy.yml.
