Privacy Policy

Boulder Tracker · Last updated 4 November 2026

In short: Boulder Tracker has no accounts, no server, no ads and no analytics. Your training data stays on your device. If you switch on sync, a copy is kept in a private folder in your own Google Drive. The developer never receives any of your data.

Who is responsible

Data stored on your device

Everything you enter is stored locally in the app's storage on your device (or, for the web version, in your browser's storage): training sessions and plans, the places you train at (names, optional coordinates, price, opening hours and notes you type), exercises, notes, goals and trips, benchmark results, outdoor sends, and daily metrics you choose to log such as sleep score, heart-rate variability (HRV), resting heart rate, bodyweight and pain entries. Some of this is health information; it is used only to show your own charts and readiness score in the app.

Uninstalling the app (or clearing the browser's site data) deletes it. Backups you export are files you create and keep yourself.

Phone calendar (optional, Android)

If you allow it under Settings → Data & connections → Phone calendar, the app asks Android for permission to read your calendars and shows their events beside your sessions on the Plan calendar. It only reads (it never writes to your calendar) and only for the days on screen. The events – titles, times, places, notes and which calendar they belong to – are read when needed, kept in memory, and are not saved in the app's data, backups or Drive sync. You can withdraw the permission at any time in Android's app settings or switch the feature off in the app.

The one exception is the AI prompts, and only if you switch it on: see "AI prompts" below. It is off by default.

Google Drive sync (optional, Android)

Sync is off until you connect a Google account under Settings → Data & connections → Sync & backup. When you connect, the app asks Google for:

  • Its own app folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata) – a hidden folder only this app can use. The app cannot see, open or change any other file in your Drive.
  • Your email address – only to show which account is connected.

The app stores a copy of your training data in that folder (one file per device) so your phone and tablet can stay in step. The data goes directly between your device and Google; the developer has no access to it. Google processes it under the Google Privacy Policy.

You can disconnect at any time in the app. To delete the synced copy, open Google Drive on the web → Settings → Manage apps → Boulder Tracker → Delete hidden app data. To remove the app's access to your account, visit myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Boulder Tracker's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to provide sync to you, is not transferred to anyone else, is not used for advertising, and is not read by any person.

Imported activities (optional)

Under Settings → Data & connections → Import activities you can pick a Strava archive, a Garmin data export or single FIT, GPX, TCX or CSV files. The files are read on your device only; nothing is uploaded. For each activity the app keeps its title, type, date and time, duration, distance, elevation, heart rate, calories, your description and, if it was recorded with GPS, a simplified line of the route (a few hundred points; the full recording is not kept). From the recording the app also works out a few small numbers and keeps only those: the time of each kilometre of a run, walk or hike, how pace and heart rate differ between the first and second half, seconds at each heart rate, a workout’s own laps, cadence, power, temperature and descent, and, from a Garmin export, your heart-rate zone times, the label Garmin gave the activity, the rating and feel you entered on the watch and the Body Battery it cost. These are stored like the rest of your data, in backups and, if you turned on sync, in your own Google Drive app folder. A Garmin data export additionally gives one value per day (sleep score and hours, HRV, resting heart rate, steps, average stress, Body Battery, breathing rate asleep, deep and REM sleep minutes, VO2max, weight); those are stored the same way, as daily values like the ones you type yourself, and are marked as imported from Garmin. The rest of the export (for example your profile, device lists, messages, courses, payments) is not read or kept. The route is drawn by the app itself, with no map service involved, so no location is sent anywhere. Nothing is sent to the developer or used for anything but showing it to you. You can remove one activity, or all of them, in the app. If you leave "Keep the full Garmin data in the app" switched on, the app also keeps every row of the export's data files exactly as Garmin wrote it (for example activity summaries including their start and end positions and place names, daily summaries, sleep, heart-rate variability, skin temperature, Body Battery, hydration, goals), so that nothing is lost for later. They are stored like the rest of your data (in backups and, if you turned on sync, in your own Google Drive app folder), only on your devices, and can be removed under Settings → Import activities. Your Garmin profile, contacts, devices, courses and the recordings themselves are not kept.

Maps behind routes (optional)

By default an imported activity's route is drawn as a plain line and the app uses no map service. If you choose Street, Terrain or Satellite on an activity (or switch on Settings → Data & connections → Import activities → Maps), the app fetches the few small map pictures under that route straight from a free public map service: OpenStreetMap (street), OpenTopoMap (terrain) or EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless (satellite). Like any website, those services receive your IP address and the map area being viewed, and the app sends nothing else (no account, no activity data). Their own privacy policies and terms apply. Choose Route, or leave the setting off, to use no map service.

Health Connect (optional, Android)

If you connect Health Connect under Settings → Data & connections → Health Connect, the app reads the data types you allow there: resting heart rate, weight and sleep sessions. It never writes to Health Connect. From them it stores one value per day on your device (resting heart rate, bodyweight and hours asleep), which feed your charts and readiness score, the same as values you enter yourself. If you asked for past data, earlier readings are imported too.

This data stays on your device and, if you turned on sync, in your own Google Drive app folder, as described above. It is not sent to the developer or anyone else, not used for advertising, and not used for anything but showing it to you. You can withdraw access at any time in Health Connect's settings; values already imported stay until you delete them.

App updates (Android)

The Android app is not distributed through an app store. It checks for newer versions by downloading a small version file from bouldertracker.nathanzumbusch.de (GitHub Pages) when it starts and at most every six hours after that, and offers an update when there is one. You can turn the automatic check off, and choose between the Stable and Testing channels, under Settings → About & Help → App updates. If you install an update, the app downloads the APK from the same address and checks it against the checksum in the version file before Android installs it. These requests carry no data from the app, but, as with any web request, GitHub sees your IP address.

Error log and feedback

If something goes wrong, the app records the error message in a log on your device (Settings → About & Help → Errors & warnings). It is never sent anywhere by the app. Under Report a bug or send feedback you can open a prefilled email or GitHub issue with your app version, device type and, only if you tick the box, the latest log entries. Nothing is sent until you send that message yourself, and the entries can mention what you were doing, so read them first.

Weather (optional)

If you give a place coordinates (your home, a crag or any other place under Settings → Places, weather & outdoor) or a trip a location, the app requests forecasts from Open-Meteo, sending the coordinates of those places, and the place names you search for when looking up a city. Places without coordinates, and everything else you enter about a place, are never sent. As with any web request, Open-Meteo also sees your IP address. No other data is sent. See Open-Meteo's terms and privacy information.

AI prompts

By default the AI features send nothing themselves: they copy a text prompt to your clipboard, and you decide whether and where to paste it into an AI service of your choice, whose own terms and privacy policy then apply. Under Settings → AI & coach → What the AI sees you choose which kinds of data a prompt may include. Health data (readiness metrics, pain) and your phone calendar are off by default. A prompt can contain your recent sessions and plan, notes you wrote, goals, and – each with its own switch – your places (names, kind, price, opening hours and notes, never their coordinates) and, if you turn it on, the titles and times of your phone-calendar events in the weeks being planned (never where they are, their notes, the guests or which calendar they are in). Two more switches cover training that isn't climbing (runs, rides, walks and workouts imported from Strava or Garmin: one line per week or each recent activity, as you choose; for each listed activity you can switch on or off its pace and kilometre splits, heart-rate zones, what Garmin says the activity trained with your rating, and climb, cadence and stride, never the route) and lines derived from your own history (which recovery signals go with your better sessions, and a short review of each recent week). Sleep, HRV and similar daily values are sent only if readiness and daily metrics are switched on, at the detail you choose (brief, a line per week, or day by day for two weeks), with VO2max and deep and REM sleep each on its own switch. The kept Garmin archive is never sent. "Ask the AI about this" on a single imported activity builds a prompt from that activity and the few before it. None of it is sent unless you paste the prompt or confirm a direct request.

Optionally, under Settings → AI & coach → Direct AI connection, you can enter your own API key for Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI or Google (Gemini). The app then sends the prompt from your device straight to that provider – only after you have seen the estimated cost and confirmed – and shows you the reply. The developer is not involved and never sees the key, the prompt or the reply. The key is stored on your device only, apart from your other data: it is not part of backups, settings files or Drive sync. The provider's terms and privacy policy apply to what you send it.

Notifications

Reminders and timer alerts are scheduled on your device by the operating system. Nothing is sent to a server.

The web version

The web version and these pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log technical data such as your IP address when you visit; see the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app does not use cookies or tracking.

Your rights

Because the developer does not receive or store your data, there is nothing held about you that could be accessed, corrected or deleted on our side – you control it in the app and in your Google account. For data held by Google, GitHub or Open-Meteo, their policies above apply. Under the GDPR you may also lodge a complaint with a data protection authority. Questions: info@nathanzumbusch.de.
